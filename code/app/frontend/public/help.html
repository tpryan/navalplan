Overview

NavalPlan is an invite-only sailing voyage planner that combines interactive maps with AI-driven research. The planning flow follows four natural stages — from exploring regions to post-sail passage debriefs.

explore
1 — Discover Browse curated sailing destinations worldwide, filtered by season, to find the right cruising area for your trip.
auto_awesome
2 — Pilot Research Once you have an area in mind, use the Virtual Harbourmaster to surface the best anchorages, marinas, moorings, fuel docks, and resource hubs within it.
location_on
3 — Plan Stops & Passages Build a multi-day itinerary or a single-day trip, mark offshore transit days as at-sea passages, and import planned GPX routes.
article
4 — Research, Sail & Debrief Fetch weather, tides, hydrographic notes, and NGA safety alerts for every stop, switch to Sailing Mode underway, and upload recorded GPX tracks for an AI passage debrief.

Getting Started

NavalPlan uses Google sign-in for authentication. Access is invite-only — you must be invited by an administrator before you can log in.

The app before login — the map is explorable, with Sign in and map view controls in the top-right corner.

  1. 1
    Sign in — Click Sign in in the top-right corner (or in the sidebar greeting) and authenticate with your Google account.
  2. 2
    Customize your map view — Use the floating control pill in the top-right corner to toggle Satellite View (satellite_alt) or switch between Light & Dark Mode (dark_mode) for night-watch comfort.
  3. 3
    Explore destinations — Click Discover to browse sailing regions worldwide and find the right area for your trip.
  4. 4
    Create a voyage or day trip — Navigate the map to your area and click + New Voyage. Choose Multi-Day Voyage or Day Trip, set a title, location, and search radius, and add dates when you know your window.
  5. 5
    Research & build your route — Run Local Pilot Research to discover hubs and anchorages, add stops to your itinerary, and run Stop Research for full weather, tide, and facility briefings.
lightbulb When you create a new voyage, a quick-start prompt lets you jump straight into Research Hubs & Spots or Select Stops Manually on the map.

Discovery Mode

Discovery Mode is the starting point for most sailors. It lets you browse hundreds of curated sailing destinations worldwide before committing to a specific route — filter by month to find where conditions are best for your planned travel window.

Discovery Mode with colored region dots worldwide. Use the month selector at the bottom to filter by sailing season.

  1. 1
    Click Discover in the sidebar (or navigate to /discover) to enter Discovery Mode. The map fills with colored region dots.
  2. 2
    Click any month in the month selector strip at the bottom of the map to filter destinations by sailing season. The dots update to show regions with ideal conditions for that month.
  3. 3
    Click any dot to open a Region Briefing — cruising conditions, highlights, charter links, entry requirements, and seasonal notes (each region also has a shareable URL at /discover/:region).
  4. 4
    When you find a region you like, close the briefing and create a voyage in that area.

Voyages & Day Trips

A voyage is the container for your trip. NavalPlan supports both multi-day cruising itineraries and single-day sails via the Multi-Day Voyage / Day Trip toggle.

The floating New Voyage card — drag it anywhere on screen while previewing and resizing the sky-blue search circle live on the map.

  1. 1
    Choose a trip mode — Click + New Voyage and select Multi-Day Voyage (start + end date range, numbered daily stops) or Day Trip (a single date and a streamlined single-stop card).
  2. 2
    Set the starting location — Type a place name to geocode it automatically, or pan the map and click Use Map Center. You can drag the floating modal by its top grip (drag_indicator) to inspect the map underneath.
  3. 3
    Adjust the search radius — Use the slider (1–150 nm) or drag the east-edge handle on the live sky-blue circle on the map. This sets the area scanned by Local Pilot Research.
  4. 4
    Add dates (optional) — Set your travel dates now or click Set Voyage Dates later in the itinerary header. In the sidebar, dated voyages are listed first (newest to oldest), followed by undated voyages alphabetically.
  5. 5
    Edit or delete — Click the edit pencil to update voyage details or resize the search circle, or click delete in the voyage list to remove a trip.
lightbulb You can reposition the search circle center or drag its east-edge resize handle directly on the map whenever the New/Edit Voyage card or Local Pilot Research is active.
auto_awesome

Local Pilot Research

Use Local Pilot Research to let the Virtual Harbourmaster scan your voyage area across 8 compass sectors and surface the best anchorages, marinas, moorings, fuel docks, and resource hubs — before you lock in your day-by-day stops.

Local Pilot Research mid-run — the radar sweep scans the area while recommendation markers stream onto the map.

  1. 1
    Click Research Hubs & Spots on a new empty voyage, or scroll to the bottom of the sidebar and click Run Local Pilot Research. This also kicks off your Destination Guide in parallel.
  2. 2
    Adjust the search area if needed — drag the center dot to reposition, or drag the east-edge handle to resize the radius. Changes save automatically.
  3. 3
    The AI scans the area and streams recommendations in real time — color-coded pins appear on the map and in the sidebar as each spot is verified.
  4. 4
    Click any marker for details and click Add as Stop to assign it to a day in your itinerary.

Use the Filter Spots bar at the bottom of the map to toggle recommendation types:

Anchorages
Moorings
Hubs & Marinas
Fuel
lightbulb Once research is complete, a Re-run Local Pilot Research button remains at the bottom of the sidebar so you can scan a shifted or resized area anytime.

Stops, Passages & Itinerary

Stops are the daily destinations in your voyage. NavalPlan supports both coastal landfall stops (harbors, marinas, anchorages) and offshore at-sea passage points for multi-day transits.

A voyage with daily stops in the sidebar itinerary and their corresponding numbered pins on the map.

  1. 1
    Add a stop — Select a day card in the itinerary, then click anywhere on the map (or click a Pilot Research marker and choose Add as Stop). The coordinates are reverse-geocoded automatically.
  2. 2
    At-sea passages & multi-day legs — For offshore transits where you are underway overnight without a landfall, a stop can be marked as an At-sea passage (rendered with a explore compass badge and a dotted route line).
  3. 3
    Add Day to Passage — Need more time between destinations? Click Add Day to Passage at the bottom of the itinerary to lengthen the voyage by one day and automatically interpolate at-sea passage points along long legs.
  4. 4
    Research a single stop — Click the science flask icon on any stop card to fetch weather, tides, hydrographic notes, and nearby facilities for that specific date. Once complete, click check_circle to view its briefing.
  5. 5
    Delete a stop — Click the delete icon on any stop card to clear that day's destination.
lightbulb When you fill the final open day in your itinerary, NavalPlan automatically clears the Pilot Research overlay and prompts you to start full Stop Research.
route

GPX Tracks & Passage Debrief

Once your voyage has at least one stop, the Tracks button (route) appears in the itinerary toolbar. Upload planned routes from your chartplotter and recorded GPS logs after sailing to compare plan vs. reality.

  1. 1
    Upload GPX files — Click Tracks in the itinerary toolbar and select a .gpx file. Choose Auto-detect (routes become Planned, timestamped track logs become Recorded), or explicitly pick Planned Route or Recorded GPS Track.
  2. 2
    Auto-split into legs — Keep Auto-split voyage into leg tracks at stops checked to automatically segment a multi-day GPX file at each itinerary stop and filter out GPS speed spikes.
  3. 3
    Inspect tracks on the map — Use the Tracks pill on the map to toggle Planned and Recorded layers. When a planned route is present, straight stop-to-stop lines are hidden cleanly. Hover over any track on the map to inspect speed, heading, and timestamps in the live HUD.
  4. 4
    Run AI Passage Debrief — When a voyage has both a planned route and a recorded track, click Debrief in the Tracks modal. The AI compares planned vs. actual distance, duration variance, average/max speed, and historical weather to produce a tactical debrief attached to each leg's starting stop in the Voyage Report.
my_location

GPS Check-in

GPS Check-in lets you log your vessel's live position during an active voyage. The check-in appears as a pulsing marker on the map and is included in the Voyage Report so shore crew and family can see your last known position.

  1. 1
    Active voyage window — The my_location check-in button in the voyage header is available when the current date is within your voyage window (from 24 hours before the start date through 24 hours after the end date).
  2. 2
    Click my_location and approve your browser's location request.
  3. 3
    NavalPlan captures your coordinates and reverse-geocodes them to the nearest locality or natural feature — or records At Sea when in open water.
  4. 4
    A pulsing marker appears on the map, and the check-in is shown in the Voyage Report as Last Captain Check-in during the active voyage window.
lightbulb Each new check-in replaces the previous one. Both the check-in button and the report callout automatically hide outside the voyage date window (±24h) so past or future planning reports stay clutter-free.

Stop Briefings & Shore Amenities

A stop briefing is a detailed AI-researched report for one location on a specific date — combining weather, tides, hydrographic pilot notes, and nearby shore amenities.

Stop Research (Pilot Agent)

Instead of researching stops one by one, use Run Stop Research to let the Pilot Agent research narrow areas around every stop and build the overall voyage guide simultaneously.

Stop research running — localized radar sweeps appear on each stop while the sidebar stepper tracks live progress.

  1. 1
    Once your daily stops are placed (connected by a dashed route line on the map), click Run Stop Research at the bottom of the sidebar. A Research Started confirmation modal appears.
  2. 2
    A localized radar sweep animation runs around each stop on the map while the sidebar stepper advances through Fetching weather forecasts → Reading tide tables → Locating nearby facilities → Building voyage guide.
  3. 3
    Nearby stops on the same date reuse shared environmental data to speed up completion. Each stop's radar sweep clears as soon as its briefing is ready.
  4. 4
    When all stops and the guide finish, a Research complete banner appears at the top of the itinerary, and you can open the full Voyage Report.
schedule Want fresher weather or tide data before departure? Click Re-run Stop Research at the bottom of the sidebar at any time.
visibility

Safety Audit & Lookout Agent

The Lookout Agent analyzes weather, sun phase, tidal currents, course headings, and live NGA Maritime Safety Information (MSI) broadcast warnings across your itinerary to alert you to changing conditions and calculate leg-by-leg departure windows.

  1. 1
    Run Stop Research first — the Lookout Agent evaluates your researched stop briefings, leg bearings, and coordinates.
  2. 2
    Trigger a fresh audit from either location:
    • Open any stop briefing and click Safety Audit in the modal header.
    • Open the Voyage Report and click the visibility eye icon in the report header.
  3. 3
    Each stop in the Voyage Report includes a Safety Lookout card with course-aware wind/wave advisories (e.g., headwinds along your exact compass bearing) and a Travel Plan table showing leg distance (NM), travel time at 4–8 kt, and the latest Depart by time to arrive before sunset.
  4. 4
    Voyage-wide alerts also appear in the Safety Overview block of the Voyage Report, grouped by stop with severity badges and recommended actions.
warning Switch the Voyage Report into Sailing Mode (sailing) while underway to put each leg's Safety Lookout advisories and Travel Plan speed/departure table front and center.

Destination Guide

The Destination Guide is an AI-compiled regional briefing covering the cruising ground's Overview, a 12-month Sailing Season calendar (color-coded for Best vs. Storm months), Regional Hazards with cited sources (Refs), At a Glance facts (country, timezone, language, currency), and Charter Info with direct links to local charter companies.

The Destination Guide — regional overview, seasonal calendar, hazards, and charter links.

  1. 1
    Click Guide in the itinerary toolbar to open the standalone Destination Guide modal, or view it embedded directly inside the Voyage Report.
  2. 2
    To refresh the guide after changing your voyage area or dates, click Redo inside the guide modal.

Voyage Report & Sailing Mode

The Voyage Report compiles your entire trip into a single document: top-level summary metrics (Stops, Days, Avg Wind, Researched), an interactive route map, a Day by Day card grid (with temp, wind, sunrise/sunset, leg distance in NM, and mini tide curves), the full Destination Guide, and detailed daily stop briefings.

The Voyage Report — route map, Day-by-Day overview cards with mini tide curves, Destination Guide, and full daily briefings.

  1. 1
    Click Report in the itinerary toolbar to open the Voyage Report.
  2. 2
    Toggle Sailing Mode (sailing) in the report header when underway. Sailing Mode hides pre-trip reference sections (Destination Guide, shore facility directories, overview cards) and highlights each stop's Safety Lookout advisories, Travel Plan speed/departure table (4–8 kt), hourly weather strip, and 24-hour tide chart.
  3. 3
    Use the report header icon bar to Share a public link (share), toggle Sailing Mode (sailing), run a Safety Audit (visibility), Copy to Clipboard (content_copy), or open the Print View (print).
lightbulb Sailing Mode has its own direct URL (/voyages/:id/report/sailing) so you can bookmark or keep it open in the cockpit while underway.
share

Sharing

Share a read-only view of your full Voyage Report with crew members, family, or float-plan contacts — no login required on their end.

  1. 1
    Open the Voyage Report (or Destination Guide) and click the share share icon in the modal header.
  2. 2
    A unique public link (/share/:token) is generated and copied. Anyone with the link can view the full report — including the map, daily briefings, tide curves, and any active GPS check-in — without signing in.
  3. 3
    To revoke access at any time, click the share icon again to disable the shared link.
lock Shared links are strictly read-only. Recipients cannot edit your voyage, upload tracks, or trigger AI research.

Using NavalPlan on Mobile

NavalPlan is built for phones and tablets in the cockpit as well as desktop planning at home.

bottom_navigation
Map / List Tab Bar Use the bottom navigation bar to switch cleanly between the interactive Map and your List (itinerary & voyages) with one thumb.
open_in_full
Full-Screen Modals & Sailing Mode Briefings and the Voyage Report expand to full screen on mobile; toggle Sailing Mode (sailing) for high-contrast, essential-only cockpit reference.
dark_mode
Satellite & Night Watch Use the top-right pill to flip between vector charts and Satellite View, or enable Dark Mode to preserve night vision underway.
offline_bolt
Service & AI Status Banner Status banners alert you if your connection drops or if the AI model is temporarily busy (503) so you know when to retry research.
lightbulb Complete your full Stop Research and Safety Audit before leaving shore coverage — once generated, briefings, tide charts, and the Voyage Report are fast to load on any device.
